Strategic Relationship Map

Where Sikka Threatens

  • MCP server enables anyone to build practice dashboards on their data
  • 50K+ practice data gives instant benchmarking capability
  • 400+ PMS connections is a 3-5 year head start
  • AI agents could automate what PF surfaces as "problems"
  • They could acquire or fund a DI competitor

Where We Coexist

  • Both believe in data normalization as foundational
  • Both serve the dental partner ecosystem
  • Both have multi-tenant data models
  • Sikka's API could power PF's Dentrix/Eaglesoft integrations
  • Sikka's MCP server could extend PF's partner capabilities

Where PracticeFront Wins

  • Practice-owner UX — Sikka has zero consumer-grade experience
  • Vendor accountability — nobody tracks partner performance
  • Curated marketplace — quality over quantity partner selection
  • "10-second scan" — Sikka sells complexity, PF sells simplicity
  • Trust engine positioning — practices control their data story

Strategic Assessment

Sikka and PracticeFront operate at different layers of the same stack. Sikka is the data pipe (infrastructure layer). PracticeFront is the intelligence and action layer (application layer). Today, they don't compete.

The risk is Sikka moving up-stack — building a practice-facing dashboard. But their DNA is developer-focused infrastructure, and their MCP server strategy actually deepens that positioning by making it easier for others to build on top of them.

The opportunity is potentially using Sikka as an integration accelerant for Dentrix/Eaglesoft coverage while PracticeFront owns the sync for OpenDental (where you control the full experience). This "own your core, partner for reach" strategy lets you move fast without building 400 PMS integrations.

The moat Sikka cannot replicate: partner accountability, curated marketplace, and the "10-second health scan" UX philosophy. These require product opinion, not data volume. Sikka sells pipes. You sell trust.
